# Rationale — Small-Fleet Trucking Primary Auto Liability Rater

**Author:** Sophia · **Status:** v0.1. QCMobile and vPIC schemas verified live 2026-09-09 (raw responses in `data/raw/`); census and crash files archived and the frequency tables built; 45 real carriers enriched and book-checked. Remaining VERIFY markers are the rate anchors in §5 (A1, A2, A7, A8, A12) and the open rows in §3.

## 1. The answer

The system prices for-hire trucking carriers with 1–5 power units for $1m CSL primary auto liability on a **power-unit-year** exposure base, enriched from FMCSA (QCMobile) and NHTSA (vPIC), and returns one of **price / refer / decline** with a full breakdown. At current parameters the base loss cost is **$4,151 per unit-year** (0.045 crashes × 2.0 claims per crash × $42,265 limited mean × 1.091 trend). Since 2026-09-09 the crash rate is the census × crash-file estimate and the claims-per-crash ratio is selected so that the product matches the market back-out; the re-basing moved the base loss cost by −1.4% and cutting the severity trend from 8% to 6% took a further −2.75% (§5 A1–A2, A8). Written unit premiums run from the **$8,000 floor to ≈$17k** (max $16,523 on the real draw, $17,400 on the synthetic sample); the median written unit premium is **≈$11,800 on the real draw** and ≈$8,900 on the synthetic sample. The floor binds for 4 of 34 written real carriers, 8 of 17 written synthetic ones and 77% of the synthetic backtest book — for clean established one-truck risks the floor, not the rate model, sets the price. On the deliberately adversarial 31-submission synthetic sample the book check prints 12 priced / 5 referred / 13 declined / 1 unreadable file. On the 45-carrier stratified census draw (15 each at <1y / 1–3y / 3y+ authority, ten focus states; `analysis/build_sample_set.py` → `data/derived/book_check_real.csv`) it prints **17 priced / 17 referred / 11 declined: decline rate 24.4%, refer rate 37.8%** — declines inside the 15–30% I expected; the refer rate is R07 (no active for-hire authority, 16 carriers) and R09 (classification not yet on file, 6), i.e. new applicants waiting on a BMC-91 filing, not data failures (R02 fired zero times).

Would it make money? Only if selection works. The margin is in the decline list and the new-venture surcharge, not in the base rate: industry commercial auto has run above 100% combined for most of a decade, and the small-fleet segment is where the worst experience sits. Section 6 gives the implied loss ratio (≈52% on the 1,000-policy portfolio, ≈46% on the 20k-policy backtest — both below the 57% permissible pure loss ratio because the floor binds), the 1-in-200 capital (≈25% of premium under independence) and the return on capital (≈28%) — all of which are conditional on the claim-frequency anchor in §5 being right.

## 2. Scope and definitions

- **Coverage:** primary auto liability, $1,000,000 combined single limit, occurrence, annual term. Federal minimum is $750k (49 CFR 387.9; confirmed by the live QCMobile record, `bipdRequiredAmount` = 750 for general-freight for-hire carriers) but shippers and brokers require $1m; ILFs for $750k / $2m are produced by `analysis/fit_severity.py`.
- **Not priced:** physical damage, cargo, GL, non-trucking liability, hazmat, passenger, HHG, auto-hauler. These route to decline D11 (appetite), not to an error.
- **Segment:** interstate authorised-for-hire property carriers, 1–5 power units, Class 7–8, general freight (dry van / reefer / flatbed and similar). Private carriers and intrastate-only are declined (D12) — they need different data and different pricing. D12 needs positive evidence: a registration under ~60 days old comes back from QCMobile with an empty operation-classification list, and that is treated as unknown (refer R09), not as private. No active MC authority with no BI/PD filing on record refers (R07): a new applicant cannot get authority until an insurer files the BMC-91, so that is what a legitimate prospect looks like at quote time. Authority that was granted and is now inactive, with no filing, is a lapse and declines (D25).
- **Submission schema:** `config/schema.json`. Only `usdot` is required; everything else is coerced, defaulted, and flagged. Defaults are conservative (unknown radius → intermediate, unknown commodity → 1.10).
- **Decision policy:** decline rules are hard; refer rules return a price but flag for human review. Refer is what stops thin-data carriers from being declined wholesale.

## 4. Method

**Exposure base.** Power-unit-year. It is the market convention, verifiable against the census record, collectable in a 60-second form, and not gameable downward (fewer declared units than census → D31). Mileage is self-reported and often stale, so it enters as a discounted relativity; revenue is unverifiable for a two-truck company. Alternatives table in `docs/DEFEND.md` §1.4.

**Frequency.** DOT-recordable crashes per power-unit-year for the segment, from census × crash file, via a negative-binomial GLM with `log(unit-years)` offset and factors fleet size / authority age / state (`analysis/build_frequency_tables.py`). The base-segment rate is the estimated value since 2026-09-09: 0.045 per unit-year (measured 0.0363 for 2–5 units with 3y+ authority, grossed up for the 19.8% of recordable crash rows that carry no DOT number), range [0.036, 0.055]. Converted to liability claims by a crash-to-claim ratio of 2.0 (adds non-recordable third-party claims, removes clearly not-at-fault crashes). Only the product of the two is identified: backing claim frequency out of the ten market price points in `docs/MARKET_BENCHMARK.md` gives 1.2–3.4 claims per crash (median 2.3), industry combined ratios above 100% make that a floor, and 2.0 holds the base loss cost within 1.4% of the pre-change value. So the change re-bases the anchor on data without moving prices; the crash-to-claim ratio is now the single most price-moving assumption (§5). The GLM's authority-age and venue relativities were **not** adopted — the census cannot separate registered from operating carriers, so they measure dormancy, not risk (`docs/FREQUENCY_PROPOSAL_2026-09-09.md` §2, §4).

**Carrier-own experience.** Bühlmann credibility, Z = n/(n+25) with n = units × 2 years. A one-truck carrier with one crash gets Z ≈ 0.07 and a ~15% surcharge; the decline rule D20 (credibility-weighted rate > 3× segment) can only fire for larger fleets with repeated crashes. This is deliberate: single crashes on tiny fleets are noise.

**Severity.** Three-component mixture on the crash flags the public data does carry — property-damage-only (lognormal, mean $18k), injury (lognormal, mean $140k, CV 2.5), fatality (Pareto above $250k, α = 1.6) — limited at the policy limit by Monte Carlo (`rater/losscost.py`). Shares per claim are 81.7% / 17.0% / 1.35%: the crash file's 34% injury and 2.7% fatal shares per recordable crash, divided by the 2.0 claims-per-crash ratio on the assumption that the extra non-recordable claims are property-damage-only. Limited mean at $1m ≈ $42k. Severity trended at 6% p.a. for 1.5 years (was 8%; the ATRI-derived series in the trade press run 3.9–6.4%, §5 A8).

**Relativities.** Multiplicative, product capped to [0.60, 3.25]. Authority age is the largest (new venture 1.65×), then venue state (high 1.35×), radius (long haul 1.30×), then OOS ratios, BASICs, driver/unit ratio, vehicle age, commodity, MCS-150 staleness, mileage intensity. Each is tagged [S] selected or [B] benchmark-anchored in `config/rates.yaml`; the GLM output replaces the first three with [E]. Two stacking controls added 2026-09-09 after the market benchmark (`docs/MARKET_BENCHMARK.md`): the product of authority × venue × radius is capped at 2.0 (uncapped it reached 2.75 on a first-year Texas long-hauler, $20.9k against a published $8–20k band), and mileage intensity is not applied when radius is already long haul (both proxy the same exposure and fired together on 4 of 10 benchmark profiles).

**Gross-up.** Loss cost × (1 + 14% ALAE) ÷ (1 − 22% expense − 6% reinsurance − 7% profit/capital) → technical unit premium; floored at **$8,000 per unit** (raised from $6,500 on 2026-09-09: every 2025–26 published source puts clean established one-truck liability at $7,500 or more, `docs/MARKET_BENCHMARK.md`); + $250 policy fee. The floor binds for roughly half of a synthetic book — for one-truck clean risks the rate model is not what sets the price.

## 6. Sensitivity, backtest, portfolio

- **Tornado** (`analysis/sensitivity.py` → `data/derived/tornado.csv`, reference carrier $13,059): crash→claim ratio (−$4.8k / +$9.0k over the market back-out band) ≫ crash rate (−$2.6k / +$2.8k over the estimated band) ≈ injury severity (−$2.1k / +$2.9k) > fatal share (−$0.8k / +$2.4k) ≈ expense ratio (−$0.9k / +$1.8k) ≈ severity trend (−$0.5k / +$1.5k). Tail α moves the $1m price by +$0.2k / −$0.3k — the limit truncates it. The crash rate fell from first to second place because its swing range is now the estimate's evidence band rather than a guess; the uncertainty moved into the conversion from crashes to claims. New-venture factor, high-venue factor and the $8k floor all show **zero** swing on this carrier because the stack cap binds (2.90 → 2.0) and the technical unit premium ($12.8k) sits above the floor; their uncapped swings are in §5 (±$2.2k, ±$1.6k). Say this out loud: *at primary $1m the claim-frequency anchor — now specifically claims per crash — is the risk; the tail becomes the risk at $2m+.*
- **ILFs** (`analysis/fit_severity.py` → `data/derived/ilf_table.csv`): limited mean at $1m $42,265; mixture gives ILF(750k) 0.966, ILF(2m) 1.058, ILF(5m) 1.094 (single lognormal: 0.967 / 1.060 / 1.098). Slightly thinner than before the re-basing (ILF(2m) was 1.064) because the per-claim mix shifted toward PDO. **That is thinner than market trucking ILFs (~1.3–1.5 at $2m)** — a signal that the fatal share or tail is too light. Flagged for calibration, not hidden.
- **Synthetic backtest** (`analysis/synthetic_backtest.py`): 20k policies, 59,862 unit-years, 5,568 claims → simulated pure loss ratio 0.464 vs rate-implied 0.473 — pipeline is sound (>0.02 apart would mean it is broken). 76.6% of policies sit at the minimum premium, which is why both are below the 57% permissible pure loss ratio (65% loss+ALAE ÷ 1.14). At 2k policies it swings ±0.10: a real 2,000-policy book moves 10 LR points on a handful of limit losses.
- **Portfolio** (`analysis/portfolio.py`): 1,000 policies, 2,950 unit-years → premium $23.6m, expected loss $12.2m (LR 0.52); aggregate loss p50 $12.1m, p90 $14.9m, p99 $17.4m, 1-in-200 $18.1m; capital (1-in-200 minus mean) $5.9m = 25% of premium; profit load $1.65m → ROC 27.9%. (Before the re-basing: LR 0.54, capital 26%, ROC 27%; more, smaller claims make the aggregate slightly less volatile.) Independence assumed — auto liability events are far less correlated than cyber, but severity *trend* is fully correlated across the book and is the systemic risk here.

## 7. Where it is most likely wrong, and what to watch in force

1. **No driver data.** Small-fleet trucking loss is mostly driver loss; I only see driver count and whatever the submission declares. First in-force addition: MVR + PSP pulls on bind.
2. **Crash → claim conversion is selected, not observed.** It is now the largest single swing in the tornado (2.0 chosen inside a 1.2–3.4 market back-out). Watch claims-per-crash on the bound book from month one; at 3.4 the reference carrier is $9k dearer.
3. **Severity tail and venue** — ILFs look thin (§6: 1.058 at $2m), and the per-claim mix now leans further to PDO. Watch large-loss emergence by state.
4. **Self-reported MCS-150 fields** (units, drivers, mileage). Cross-checked against VIN count and inspection activity, but a carrier that never gets inspected is invisible.
5. **Adverse selection.** An instant-quote entrant sees new ventures and carriers other insurers dropped. Watch quote-to-bind mix by authority-age bucket weekly; if <1-year carriers exceed their census share by 2×, the new-venture factor is too low.
6. **Insurance-cancellation signal** currently comes from the submission, not L&I. Automate L&I before go-live.
7. **Market-benchmark watch list (not applied, evidence too thin — one named renewal each):** a tenure credit above 3 years (authority age gives no credit beyond 3 yrs, so a 39-year carrier prices like a 4-year one); vehicle age 1.20 on 20+ year trucks (old iron matters more for physical damage than third-party loss); The third watch-list item, the 8% severity trend against ATRI's 4–6% premium trend, was acted on the same day: trend cut to 6% (§5 A8). Revisit the other two with bound-book data.

## 8. What this attracts, and roadmap

**Book attracted:** small, mostly newer, general-freight carriers in mid-venue states, priced $7–15k per unit; high-venue long-haul new ventures priced ~$15–16k after the stack cap (was $20k+), inside the published $8–20k band; clean established owner-operators at the $8k floor, at the bottom of the published $7.5–11k range. Declines concentrate on authority/status, appetite and data-integrity rules.

**Roadmap:** (1) A1 and A7 re-based on the crash file (done 2026-09-09); the GLM's A3/A5 values were rejected as dormant-registration artefacts and need an L&I grant date or first-inspection date as the "started operating" marker before retrying; the solo-fleet relativity 1.18 [E] is proposed but not applied; (2) L&I automation; (3) MVR/PSP at bind; (4) telematics discount; (5) physical damage using vPIC stated value; (6) re-fit relativities on 12 months of quote-to-bind and claims; (7) price elasticity by segment.
